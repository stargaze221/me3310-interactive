What should you notice?

  • The true calibration relationship is unchanged across repetitions.
  • Random measurement variation produces different finite datasets.
  • Different datasets produce different estimates of the slope and intercept.
  • Increasing n usually makes the distribution of estimated slopes narrower.
Optional Insight: Monte Carlo and the Curse of Dimensionality

Suppose you try to cover a parameter space with the same number of grid values in every dimension. The required number of combinations grows exponentially.

Equivalent MC samples per dimension100

10,000 Monte Carlo samples in 2 dimensions correspond to about 100 samples per dimension.

Monte Carlo avoids exhaustive grid enumeration, but it does not make a high-dimensional space dense. High-dimensional spaces remain sparse.
