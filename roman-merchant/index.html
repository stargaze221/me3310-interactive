The Cost of Being Certain

When a familiar route becomes dangerous, is it enough to be risk-aware?

Imagine you are a merchant in the Roman Empire. You have two possible trade routes. Road A has served you well for years. Sea Route B is unfamiliar and has a poor reputation. You have 100 gold coins to start.

One Decision. Three Worlds.

Model assumptions · Equations, noise distributions, and bias–variance

A simplified stochastic decision model. Merchant rewards, reporting noise, and belief updates are independent pieces of the model. Everything below reflects the actual JavaScript simulation—not historical measurements.

1 · Actual trade outcome

Ordinary profit is Gaussian, with a small probability of a fixed catastrophic loss:

Xi,t ∼{ Normal(mi,si2)with probability 1−pi Liwith probability pi

The mean of the mixture is:

μi=(1−pi)mi+piLi

Crucial: “Normal profit” refers to the usual outcome only. The overall outcome distribution has a rare-loss tail.

2 · Reported information

Trade reports are corrupted by additive Gaussian noise and possible systematic bias:

Yi,t=Xi,t+bi,t+ηi,t ηi,t∼N(0,22)

Noise SD = 2 coins. In War + Rumors, Road A reports have bias = +7 coins after the shift; otherwise bias is zero.

3 · Estimated return and risk

Both strategies learn from their reported observations using a slowly updated estimate:

Qi+=Qi+αt(Yi,t−Qi) vi+=max⁡{1,(1−αt)vi+αt(Y−Q)2}

Normally α = 0.03. In War + Rumors, if a Road A report is worse than its current estimate, α = 0.006 instead: a simplified confirmation filter.

Here v is a smoothed squared-surprise proxy, not an unbiased estimate of the true return variance.

4 · Risk-aware choice

Both strategies choose the route with the highest estimated risk-adjusted score:

Si=Qi−0.6vi

The exploitation strategy stops here: it only receives reports from the route it selects.

The scouting strategy also buys an independent signal about B at decisions 1, 5, 9, …:

Zt=μB(t)+ξt,ξt∼N(0,1.22)

Scouting costs 1.8 coins each time, sharply updates the belief about B (weight 0.70), and does not replace that decision's normal trade.

5 · Regret (opportunity loss)

gt=maxi∈{A,B}μi,t −μat,t +cscout

Scouting cost is zero for exploitation and 1.8 on scouting decisions. After bankruptcy, trading stops and the simulation counts foregone positive best-route earnings instead.

6 · Experimental controls held fixed

52 decisions per merchant; crisis after decision 20. 500 paired simulations, same underlying route-outcome sequences across strategies; initial wealth 100; bankruptcy if wealth falls below 20.

Both merchants start with estimated returns QA = 5 and QB = −1.5, and volatility proxies vA = 9 and vB = 36.

Regret uses hidden true means for evaluation; the merchants see only their reports and estimates. Regret and realized wealth measure different things.

Bias vs. variance: Gaussian observation noise (variance 4) makes reports fluctuate around their expected values. A +7 reporting bias shifts the average report itself, while the asymmetric confirmation filter makes favorable beliefs slower to change. More observations alone do not eliminate systematic bias.

Historical boundary: a fictional merchant around AD 220–260. Rome experienced a third-century political and military crisis beginning in AD 235. The routes and numerical parameters are illustrative, not historical reconstructions.

Interpretation: parameters were selected to make contrasts visible. Risk-aware exploitation is not guaranteed to be safe, and scouting is not guaranteed to succeed across other parameter choices or information sources. No cognitive model of confirmation bias is being empirically validated. Historical context.
