Confidence Interval & the t-Distribution

Repeated measurements give us a sample mean. A confidence interval helps us describe how precisely that sample estimates the population mean.

Lab 01 connection

Estimate the mean from a finite sample

In Lab 01, the population standard deviation is not known. We estimate variability using the sample SD, so we use the t-distribution for the introductory confidence interval for the mean.

Standard error SE = s / √n

Why is the t-interval wider? With a small sample, we are estimating both the mean and the variability from limited data. The t-distribution accounts for that extra uncertainty. As n grows, t approaches the normal distribution and the two intervals become nearly identical.

Three quantities that are easy to confuse

sHow variable are the individual measurements?
s / √nHow variable is our estimate of the mean?
t* · s / √nHow far do we extend from the sample mean for this confidence interval?

Think before calculating

If the sample SD stays about the same, why does collecting more measurements usually narrow the confidence interval?

A narrow confidence interval tells us the mean is estimated precisely. Does that alone prove the experiment is unbiased or that the engineering model is correct?
