Deeper connection

Why do we square the residuals?

For residuals \(r_i=V_i-(b_0+b_1p_i)\), least squares chooses the parameters that minimize

\(\mathrm{SSE}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_i^2\)
Gaussian noise and maximum likelihood

If independent measurement errors are modeled as Gaussian with constant standard deviation, \(\varepsilon_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\), then

\(\log L=\mathrm{constant}-\dfrac{\mathrm{SSE}}{2\sigma^2}\)

Therefore, maximizing the likelihood is equivalent to minimizing the sum of squared residuals. This gives least squares a probabilistic interpretation—it is not merely a convenient curve-fitting rule.

Important: using least squares does not prove that real sensor errors are Gaussian. The error model is an engineering assumption that should be examined against experimental evidence.

Connect to Lab 02

From voltage back to pedal position

Once a calibration has been estimated, a new voltage measurement can be converted to an estimated pedal position using

\(\hat p=\dfrac{V-\hat b_0}{\hat b_1}\)

The remaining question is experimental, not merely algebraic: over what range, and with what evidence, should we trust that estimate?
