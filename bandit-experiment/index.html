How the simulation works (model and interpretation)

Each option i has an expected performance μi,t. On decision t, the selected option produces one observed score ri,t = μi,t + measurement noise. Noise is Gaussian with standard deviation 1.5.

Stable: the true means stay fixed. Changing: the expected rewards switch abruptly at half the experiment. Volatile: the true means follow a mean-reverting random walk, independent of the learner's actions.

Sample-average estimate: Qi is the mean of observed scores for option i. Recent-data estimate: Qi ← Qi + 0.15(r − Qi), after the first observation. For ε-Greedy, with probability 1 − ε choose the option with the largest current Q; otherwise select uniformly at random.

Expected regret: sum over decisions of maxj μj,t − μat,t. This uses the simulated hidden means for evaluation, not information available to the learner. Reported confidence intervals describe variability in the simulated total observed rewards across independent runs.

Limitation: This is a simplified multi-armed bandit. The underlying option performance changes even when an option is not selected, and there is no switching cost. Financial diversification and corporate R&D investment are analogies, not identical mathematical models.
