Coin Toss: Binomial → Normal

A single coin toss has only two outcomes. But the number of heads in many independent tosses has a binomial distribution whose shape becomes increasingly well approximated by a normal distribution.

Central Limit Idea

Increase the number of tosses

Question: How can a discrete experiment made only of heads and tails begin to look like a continuous bell-shaped distribution?

5
DistributionBinomial(5, 0.5)
Mean, np2.50
SD, √np(1−p)1.12
Normal approximationN(2.50, 1.12²)
Exact binomialNormal approximation
What to notice: the outcomes remain integer counts, but as n grows the binomial probability pattern becomes smoother and increasingly bell-shaped. For a fair coin, the matching normal model is approximately N(n/2, n/4).

Observe

Compare n = 5 with n = 30 and n = 100. Where does the normal approximation improve most?

Interpret

The coin itself never becomes normal. It is the distribution of an aggregate quantity—the number of heads—that becomes approximately normal.
