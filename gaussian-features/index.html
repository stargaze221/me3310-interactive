4. Why does the series converge?

In one dimension, the Gaussian kernel can be written as an infinite inner product. Keeping only the first N terms gives a directly testable approximation:

K(x,x′) = e−(x²+x′²)/(2σ²) ∑n=0∞ (xx′/σ²)n/n!
KN(x,x′) = e−(x²+x′²)/(2σ²) ∑n=0N−1 (xx′/σ²)n/n!

The corresponding feature is φn(x) = e−x²/(2σ²)(x/σ)n/√(n!). Its squared magnitude is e−λ λn/n!, with λ = (x/σ)².

Important distinction: for a given input, features do not always shrink immediately as the degree increases. Their magnitude increases up to around (x/σ)² and then decreases. The exact kernel avoids explicitly constructing these features.

Engineering judgment: what is enough?

Think–Pair–Discuss: Try x = 4, σ = 1. Are 10 or 20 features enough? Now increase σ to 2. Why does the answer change? Would a student get the same conclusion for new motion data outside the tested feature range?

Connection to the final project: Gaussian SVM uses the exact kernel directly. We are approximating it here only to investigate why the kernel trick is powerful. A good approximation of the mathematical kernel is not, by itself, evidence that the motion classifier is reliable.
