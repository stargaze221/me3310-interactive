The Tiger Problem

Two closed doors. One hides a tiger 🐅 and the other a treasure 💰. You cannot see the hidden state. You can pay to listen before you decide which door to open.

Listen: −1 point
Find treasure: +10
Open tiger door: −100
Default hearing accuracy: 85%

4 · How much confidence is enough?

Compare six simple stopping rules plus an Optimal POMDP policy, with 5,000 simulated rounds per policy. Listening costs points but may reduce the risk of opening the tiger door. Every policy has the same 12-listen limit.

Baseline · Open immediately

Open the left door without listening. No evidence is collected.

Exploration · Confidence threshold

Listen until the model assigns at least 60%, 70%, 80%, 90%, or 95% probability to one tiger location (maximum 12 listens); then open the safer door.

Optimal POMDP benchmark · Finite-horizon planning

Instead of using one fixed confidence threshold, the policy compares the expected value of opening now against the expected value of listening once more. It accounts for the current belief, the listening cost, and the remaining opportunities. “Optimal” means optimal for the assumed sensor model.

Run the comparison, then switch sensor scenarios and compare again.
Look for repeated results: With an assumed hearing accuracy of 85%, one observation moves the belief from 50% to 85%; two agreeing observations move it to about 97%. Different thresholds can therefore lead to exactly the same stopping behavior. A correctly calibrated 60% sensor produces more gradual belief updates.
What is being simulated?

Each round starts with a randomly placed tiger and a 50/50 prior. Each listen costs −1 and independently reports the tiger side with the scenario's actual reliability. Beliefs are updated using the scenario's assumed reliability. Opening treasure earns +10; opening the tiger door costs −100.

Each policy faces the same 5,000 hidden tiger placements and potential observation sequences. A fixed random seed makes the demonstration reproducible. Listening stops at a maximum of 12 observations even if the target confidence has not been reached.

Finite-horizon POMDP: A Bellman recursion compares opening either door to listening once more: V = max(open left, open right, −1 + expected next V). The policy is optimized for the assumed observation accuracy and at most 12 listens, with a mandatory door choice at the end. This differs from the classic infinite-horizon discounted Tiger POMDP. In the miscalibrated scenario, the policy can be optimal for its incorrect model but not optimal for the real sensor.

5 · Think – Pair – Discuss

If your model says “97% confident,” what exactly does that number depend on?

Which stopping threshold offers the best tradeoff between the cost of listening and the risk of opening the tiger door? When can more observations create more confidence without more reliability?

Engineering connection: The real state is hidden. Measurements are imperfect. A rational decision therefore depends not only on the data, but also on the model used to interpret those data.

Exploration can reveal not only a better option, but also how uncertain our current answer really is.
