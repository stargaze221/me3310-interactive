Connect the visualization to the equations

Why does the slope interval usually narrow?

For simple linear regression, the standard error of the estimated sensitivity is

\(SE_{\hat b_1}=\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_i-\bar p)^2}}\)

and the 95% confidence interval is

\(\hat b_1\pm t_{0.975,n-2}SE_{\hat b_1}\).
Two effects matter: as informative calibration points accumulate across the input range, \(\sum(p_i-\bar p)^2\) generally grows, and the Student-t critical value decreases toward about 1.96 as the degrees of freedom increase. Residual scatter \(s\) can still fluctuate, so the interval need not shrink monotonically after every point.

A subtle but important distinction

Slope interval versus confidence band

The lower chart asks, “How precisely do we know the sensor sensitivity?” The upper shaded band asks a different question: “How precisely do we know the mean calibration response at a particular pedal position?”

\(\hat V(p_0)\pm t^*s\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{n}+\dfrac{(p_0-\bar p)^2}{\sum(p_i-\bar p)^2}}\)

The band is typically narrowest near the center of the calibration inputs and wider near the edges. A prediction interval for a new individual sensor measurement would be wider still.

Experimental reasoning: More data do not magically reveal the true model. They can reduce uncertainty about parameters when the measurements are informative and the assumed model is appropriate.
