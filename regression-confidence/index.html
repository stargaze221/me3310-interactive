Connect the visualization to the equations

Both coefficients are estimated from the same data

For simple linear regression, the standard errors are

\(SE_{\hat b_1}=\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_i-\bar p)^2}}\)
\(SE_{\hat b_0}=s\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{n}+\dfrac{\bar p^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_i-\bar p)^2}}\)

and each 95% confidence interval has the form

\(\text{estimate}\pm t_{0.975,n-2}SE\).
Why keep sensitivity as the main focus? In sensor calibration, \(b_1\) has the direct physical meaning of sensitivity. The offset plot is included to make clear that \(b_0\) is also estimated—not fixed.

A subtle but important distinction

Coefficient intervals versus the confidence band

The two lower charts ask how precisely we know the parameters \(b_0\) and \(b_1\). The upper shaded band asks a different question: how precisely do we know the mean sensor response at a particular pedal position?

\(\hat V(p_0)\pm t^*s\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{n}+\dfrac{(p_0-\bar p)^2}{\sum(p_i-\bar p)^2}}\)

A prediction interval for a new individual sensor measurement would be wider still.

Experimental reasoning: More data do not magically reveal the true model. They can reduce uncertainty about estimated parameters when the measurements are informative and the assumed model is appropriate.
