Sampling Distribution of the Mean

Take many independent samples from the same population, calculate one mean from each sample, and look at the distribution of those means. This is the Central Limit Theorem in experimental form.

Question: If individual measurements are not normally distributed, can the sample mean still have an approximately normal sampling distribution?

SimulationNormal model for X̄
Central Limit Theorem: for independent, identically distributed measurements with finite variance, the standardized sample mean (X̄ − μ)/(σ/√n) approaches N(0,1) as n grows. Equivalently, for sufficiently large n, X̄ ≈ N(μ, σ²/n).
Law of Large Numbers

tells us where the sample mean goes: X̄ becomes concentrated near μ.

Central Limit Theorem

tells us about the shape and scale of the sampling distribution: approximately normal with SE = σ/√n.

Try this: start with the right-skewed population at n = 1, then compare n = 5, 10, and 30. The individual measurements remain skewed; the distribution of their averages changes.
