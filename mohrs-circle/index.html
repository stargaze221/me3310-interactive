Interpret

Engineering judgment

If you measured strain in only one direction, could you reconstruct this entire 2D strain state?

Connect to the three-element rosette

What would a 0° / 45° / 90° rosette measure?

A rectangular strain rosette samples the same local strain state in three directions. Those three measurements are enough to recover \(\epsilon_x\), \(\epsilon_y\), and \(\gamma_{xy}\) for the assumed 2D strain state.

ε0—0° gauge
ε45—45° gauge
ε90—90° gauge
\(\gamma_{xy}=2\epsilon_{45}-\epsilon_0-\epsilon_{90}\)

Equations behind the visualization

Strain transformation and principal strain

\(\epsilon_\theta=\dfrac{\epsilon_x+\epsilon_y}{2}+\dfrac{\epsilon_x-\epsilon_y}{2}\cos2\theta+\dfrac{\gamma_{xy}}{2}\sin2\theta\)
\(\dfrac{\gamma_{x'y'}}{2}=-\dfrac{\epsilon_x-\epsilon_y}{2}\sin2\theta+\dfrac{\gamma_{xy}}{2}\cos2\theta\)
\(\epsilon_{1,2}=\dfrac{\epsilon_x+\epsilon_y}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{\epsilon_x-\epsilon_y}{2}\right)^2+\left(\dfrac{\gamma_{xy}}{2}\right)^2}\)
\(\theta_p=\dfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{atan2}(\gamma_{xy},\epsilon_x-\epsilon_y)\)

Experimental reasoning: A strain gauge gives one directional measurement. A rosette provides enough directional evidence to infer the local 2D strain state, and Mohr's circle helps us interpret the principal strains and their directions.
